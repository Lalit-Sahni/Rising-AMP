Cost plan

The estimate stops being a PDF with pen marks on it and becomes the spine of the job. Three numbers on every trade: what you estimated, what you were quoted, what you have actually paid. One screen that answers "are we winning or losing, and where", on a job with a 120 line estimate and on a job with nothing but a number in your head.

The problem, stated plainly

Right now the estimate and the ledger are two different worlds

RisingAMP knows what you paid. The Bill of Quantities knows what you planned. Nothing joins them, so the only place they meet is in biro in the right margin of a printout.

What the app has today

Expenses, invoices, suppliers, files. A running total of money out. No idea whether that total is good or bad, because there is nothing to compare it to.

What the estimate has

21 trade sections, about 120 lines, quantities, unit rates. $321,916 ex GST, $354,108 with GST. A plan, frozen the day it was written.

What is missing

The middle layer. Quotes. The real prices that came back from real people, which are almost never the estimated price and almost never line up with the lines.

Found while reading your estimate. Some of your margin notes are quotes and some are just GST conversions, and there is no way to tell them apart on paper. Air-conditioning is estimated at $6,363.64 and the note says "$7000 Incl 10 kW Daikin", but 6,363.64 × 1.1 is exactly $7,000, so that note is the same number with GST on it. Painting is $10,386.36 and the note says "11425", and 10,386.36 × 1.1 is exactly $11,425. Same again. But "6500 actual asif bhai gave" on the same air-con row is a real quote, and so is "$39,500 Plus GST, Mark gave prices" on the kitchen. Four notes, two meanings, one column. That ambiguity is the thing the app should delete.

The one decision that dissolves the mess

The section is the unit of truth. The line is only the unit of the estimate.

You asked how to handle a concreter who says $30,000 for the job including piering, benching, excavation and the slab. The answer is not to make him itemise. The answer is to stop pretending quotes and bills happen at line level.

If you match at line level

You have to split $30,000 across 2.001 Benching, 2.002 Excavation, 2.003 Piering, 2.005 Slab and 2.006 Drop edge beams. He never gave you that split. So you invent one. Every number after that is fiction, and you will spend your evenings maintaining it.

This is the version that gets abandoned in three weeks.

If you match at section level

Piering, benching, excavation, slab and drop edge beams are all inside 2.000 Concreting. His $30,000 is one quote against one section. No split, no invention, no maintenance. Estimated $41,110 with GST, quoted $30,000, and you are $11,110 up on concreting.

Nothing to decide. It just lands.

The estimate lines stay. They are how you check the quote is sane, and how you argue with a concreter who forgot the pump hire. They are read only. You never tick them off, you never code an expense to one, and you never have to keep them current.

A quote that genuinely spans two trades gets split, and only then. A plumber whose price includes the driveway drainage in 18.000 gets two allocation lines. Rare, and the one place the app asks you to do arithmetic.

Every section is optional. No quote is a normal state. No expenses is a normal state. The screen is honest about not knowing rather than showing zero and implying it is finished.

The messy cases, one by one

You said it gets hella messy. Here is every mess and what happens to it.

"$30,000 including piering, benching, etc"

One quote, one section, no split. Covered above. This is the common case and it costs you nothing.

An expense that is not in the estimate at all

It goes to Not in the estimate, which is a real bucket, not an error. It adds to expected final and it is listed by name so you can see what the extras actually were. Never hidden inside Miscellaneous.

You cannot be bothered coding an expense

Uncoded is allowed and visible. Section variances stay true, only the whole-job forecast has a pool sitting outside it. The app guesses from the supplier, Rodgers to Bricks, your sparky to Electrical, and you confirm with one tap. It never guesses silently.

Three prices for the same trade

All three are quotes on that section. One is marked chosen and drives the forecast. The other two stay on file, which is how you later prove you could have had it for $30,000.

A range, "30 to 35K"

A quote can carry a low and a high. The forecast uses the high, so the number is never optimistic. The row says "$30,000 to $35,000" so you remember it is not firm.

One quote covering two trades

Split it across sections with an amount each. The form will not let the parts add to something other than the total. This is the only arithmetic the app ever asks for, and it is rare.

Plus GST or including GST

The single worst source of fake variance, and you have already hit it twice in the margins. Every quote records which it was and the app stores both. One toggle at the top of the screen switches the whole page. Your bills are GST inclusive, so that is the default.

The scope changes halfway through

The baseline never gets edited. Once the estimate is locked, a change is a new quote or a not-in-estimate cost. If you rewrite the estimate you can no longer answer "did we beat the plan", which is the only question the screen exists to answer.

The real question

It has to work on a job with no estimate at all

Kelly St has a 120 line Bill of Quantities because someone was paid to write one. Most jobs will never have that. If the feature only works when a spreadsheet exists, it is a Kelly St screen, not a feature. So the spreadsheet is the richest input, not the required one.

One number

10 seconds

"This job should cost about $340,000." That is the whole setup.

You immediately get a bar, a percentage, and a verdict: paid $69,130 of $340,000, on track. No coding, no trades, no spreadsheet. That alone is more than the app tells you today, and it works on a job that is already 124 expenses deep.

2

Trades with numbers

10 minutes

Pick trades off a standard list, type an amount on each. Twenty rows, done on a phone at the kitchen table.

Now you get per trade variance, quotes, and the whole screen from earlier. This is the level most jobs will actually reach, and it needs no file of any kind.

3

Import an estimate

Kelly St

Upload the spreadsheet. Same screen as level 2, plus the line detail sitting underneath each trade so you can check a quote against it.

Line detail is decoration on top of level 2, not the thing itself. A trade with no lines behaves identically to one with six.

One screen, four levels of effort, and you can move up whenever you feel like it. Level 1 to level 2 is typing numbers into rows that already exist. Level 2 to level 3 is an import that fills in detail under trades you already have.

The rule that keeps this out of everyone's way. A job with no cost plan behaves exactly as it does today, everywhere in the app. No tab, no empty screen, no "0 of 0" on Overview, no expense form asking for a trade. Different looking estimates

Two lists, and only one of them belongs to the estimate

Your 21 sections came from your estimator. Someone else's will have 12, with different names, in a different order. If expenses code to whatever a particular spreadsheet happened to call things, the feature falls apart the second a second estimate exists.

Trades, which the app owns

About twenty, shipped with the app, shared by every job. Site works, Concreting, Plumbing, Carpentry, Bricks, Electrical, Roofing, Windows and doors, Kitchen and joinery, Tiling and flooring, Painting, Waterproofing, HVAC, External works, Other. You can add to the list for your business, never per job.

Expenses code to a trade. Stable, comparable across jobs, and it lets the app learn that Rodgers means Bricks once and for all.

Sections, which the estimate owns

Whatever your file literally says, codes and all. "1.000 Site Works, Planning, approval and site requirements" stays exactly that, because that is what you will be reading when you argue with someone about scope.

Each section is mapped to a trade at import. That mapping is the only place the two worlds meet, and you see it before anything is saved.

Why the mapping step is not optional, from your own file. Your estimate uses the code 15.000 twice, once for Painting and once for Scaffolding. Section 14.000 is "Wardrobes, Walk-ins & Shower screens" and 16.000 is "Shower screens, fixtures & accessories", so shower screens appear in two places. There is no 10.000 at all. Item codes in a real Bill of Quantities are a human habit, not an identifier. An importer that trusts them silently produces a wrong screen, which is worse than one that asks you a question.

Why expenses code to a trade, not a section

The single most important line in the schema. Sections belong to a document that gets replaced when a revised estimate arrives. Trades do not. Code an expense to a section and re-importing wipes six months of coding. Code it to a trade and the plan can be swapped underneath it as often as you like.

Why one document, not a subcollection of lines

120 lines is roughly 20 KB, well inside Firestore's 1 MB limit. One read draws the whole screen. No fan-out, no new rules, no index. The boring option is the right one at your size.

Why quotes are separate documents

They arrive one at a time over months, they get a file attached, and they get voided rather than deleted. Same shape as invoices, same rules pattern, nothing new to learn.

Nothing derived is stored

Expected final, variance, and the trade status are all computed on read, same as margin and the verdict today. DATABASE.md item 7 already says do not store a verdict unless you define who updates it. Nobody would.

Kelly St already has a ledger. It was Gurner St and it has real expenses on it, which means the paid column is populated the moment the estimate is imported. That is the good news. The other side is that coding the existing expenses is a real backfill, done by hand, one job, one evening. Worth doing on staging first with a copy, so the tap-to-code flow gets fixed before it touches production.

The thing to decide before any of this

Kelly St is your own build, and the app assumes you are invoicing someone

Centenary has a client, invoices and a margin. Kelly St has none of those, so today the app shows it as a job with no income and no margin, which reads as broken rather than as a different kind of job.

Building for a client

The question is margin. Invoiced minus cost. The cost plan is a second opinion: are you going to make what you thought you would make.

Building for yourself

There is no invoice and no margin. The question is estimate against actual, full stop. The cost plan is not a second opinion, it is the whole scoreboard, and the Overview verdict should say so instead of showing a margin of zero.

That is one field on the job, kind: client | own, and a different verdict line on Overview. Small, but it should be decided before the cost plan lands, otherwise Kelly St gets a beautiful new screen sitting above a header that still says there is no income.

How it would ship

Five parts, smallest first, and every one of them lands on all jobs

Deliberately ordered so the import, the part that only matters to Kelly St, is not the part everything else waits on.

Part A

Trade list, target cost, and the screen

Level 1. Ship the twenty trades, let a job carry a single target number, draw the Cost plan screen against the paid total. No coding, no quotes, no file. Nothing writes to the ledger, and it works on Centenary the same day it works on Kelly St.

Part B

Break it into trades, and code expenses

Level 2. Type an amount per trade, add one optional tradeId to an expense, a tap on History, a suggestion from the supplier, and the Uncoded bucket. This is where the screen comes alive, and it still needs no spreadsheet.

Part C

Quotes

Add a quote, allocate it across one or more trades, mark one chosen, attach the PDF, record whether it included GST. Expected final becomes a forecast rather than a copy of the plan.

Part D

Import a spreadsheet

Level 3. The column mapper, the section to trade mapping screen, the totals check, line detail under each trade. Kelly St gets its 120 lines. Every other job carries on unaffected.

Part E

Job kind and what needs you

Own build versus client build. Then the signals: a trade quoted well over plan, a trade paid past its quote, an expense uncoded for two weeks, a trade with spend but no quote.

Deliberately not in this

What the cost plan is not

Not a schedule. No dates, no dependencies, no Gantt. Money only.

Not a takeoff tool. It never calculates a quantity. It reads what your estimator wrote.

Not a purchase order system. A quote is a record of a price, not a commitment you issue.

Not per-line reconciliation. Said once already, worth saying again. That is the version that dies.

Not editable after lock. The baseline is the baseline. Changes are quotes and extras.

Not a new backend service. No Cloud Functions and no new packages. New collections get narrow, membership-gated Firestore rules.

Not compulsory. A job with no plan looks exactly like it does today. No tab, no empty card, no trade field on the expense form.

Not a PDF reader. Spreadsheets and typing only. A number the app guessed wrong is worse than a number you spent a minute entering.
