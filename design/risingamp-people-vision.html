RisingAMP / Phase 18

A person is a person, not a list of email strings

Today the whole permission system is one line of code: is your email the same as the org's owner email. Everyone else on a job can do everything on it. People show up as chips under the job title, removing one means digging through an invite panel, and there is nowhere to look at anybody. This is what replaces that.

The one that matters most

A homeowner is not a Viewer

A Viewer reads the cost plan, which means supplier pricing, subcontractor rates and the margin. Never give a client this role. A client-facing view is a separate build with a different data boundary, and it is deliberately not in this phase.

Data model

Two arrays, one extra read of nothing

managers[] and viewers[] on the job document, alongside the existing invitedEmails[]. Absent from both means Site. The rules already fetch the job document once, so the role rides along in that same read and costs nothing.

Migration

Assign from evidence, not from a guess

The script reads what each person actually did in the last ninety days, expenses, invoices, files, cost plan changes, and proposes a role from that. It prints the table and writes nothing until you approve it.

B

The People page

/people

One row per person in the business, not per job. The row carries everything you would squint at a chip to find out: who they are, what they can do, which jobs they are on, and whether they have actually turned up. Attention sorts to the top, so somebody who was invited nine days ago and never signed in is the first thing you see.

D

What this design commits to

hold the build to these
CommitmentWhy, and what breaks if it slips
A person is a row Photo, name, role, jobs, last active, and what they have done. A chip cannot carry that, which is why the current one tells you nothing.
One place to change anything Invite, assign, move, remove, all from the person panel. The moment there are two places, they drift, and the one you are not looking at is the one that is wrong.
No phone numbers, no addresses toPublicProfile strips them deliberately and a comment in the code says so. That boundary was closed on purpose in an earlier phase. Do not reopen it to make this page feel fuller.
Hiding is never the enforcement The role is cached in local storage for first paint. Anybody can edit it. Every gated action must fail on the rules, and say so out loud when it does.
A refused write explains itself "You need to be a manager to send an invoice" and not a button that silently does nothing. Under four roles this will happen daily to somebody.
Attention sorts to the top The page exists to answer who needs something from me. Alphabetical would be a list; this is a worklist.

Names and figures on this page are illustrative, apart from the owner and the job names, which are real. Ravi Kumar, Deepak Sharma and Megan Tran are stand-ins for the shapes of person this has to handle: somebody invited who never arrived, somebody who only does site work, and an outside bookkeeper who must never be able to change a number.
